Korean history, side by side with the world

A free timeline app for teaching and learning Korean history. See what was happening in Korea and around the world in the same year, on one screen.

The app is in Korean. Its screens and events are written in Korean, and this site explains them in English. In the stores it appears as 한국역사연표.

No sign-up or payment. The timeline works without an internet connection.

Teach

Made in a classroom, ready for yours

The app began in a Korean classroom, as a timeline students could keep on a phone or tablet. It can also support lessons on Korean history, culture, and Hangul at schools outside Korea.

Because everything in the app is in Korean, students learning the language can read real Korean as they explore. If you or your students don't read Korean yet, start with the User Guide, which explains every screen, and the lesson ideas. A camera translation app can help with event text.

Eras

From the beginning of Earth to today

The app holds 827 events across 31 eras: 464 from Korean history and 363 from world history. (As of version 1.2.4)

  1. 4.6 billion years agoPrecambrian, the time of Earth
  2. 700,000 BCEPaleolithic culture on the Korean Peninsula
  3. 2333 BCEGojoseon
  4. 918Goryeo
  5. 1392Joseon
  6. 1443Creation of Hunminjeongeum (Hangul)
  7. 1919March First Independence Movement
  8. 1945Liberation of Korea

Events come from sources including the Korean History Database, the National Institute of Korean History, Wikipedia, the Doosan Encyclopedia, and news reports.

World

Compare Korea and the world

Tap the globe button and 363 world history events join the Korean timeline in order. Students can connect Korean history to events they already know.

Events a few years apart read as one story too. Hunminjeongeum was created in 1443, and ten years later, in 1453, the Ottoman Empire took Constantinople.

Dates

Key events are recorded to the day

464 events list the month and day as well as the year, so students can follow what came first within a single year. Here is 1945.

  1. August 6, 1945Atomic bombing of Hiroshima World
  2. August 15, 1945Liberation of Korea
  3. September 2, 1945Japan's surrender and the end of World War II World
  4. October 9, 1945Hangul Day ceremony held by the Korean Language Society
  5. October 24, 1945Founding of the United Nations World

Dates also power the home screen, which shows the events that happened on today's date.

Hangul

Follow the story of Hangul

Search for 한글 or 조선어 to trace the Korean alphabet from its creation through the colonial period.

  1. 1443King Sejong creates Hunminjeongeum, in the 25th year of his reign
  2. 1446Hunminjeongeum and its Haerye edition are proclaimed, in the 28th year of his reign
  3. December 3, 1921Hangeulmo is renamed the Korean Language Research Society
  4. November 4, 1926The first Gagyanal, marking 480 years since the proclamation
  5. January 10, 1931The society is renamed the Korean Language Society
  6. October 1, 1942Japanese police arrest members of the society
  7. October 9, 1945The society holds a Hangul Day ceremony

The app is a timeline of events rather than a Hangul course. Each event links to Wikipedia or a related source, usually in Korean, for further reading.

Daily

Tools for class and study

Find an event quickly

Search by event, person, year, or tag. Tap a result to jump to its place on the timeline. Search terms need to be in Korean, though years work as numbers.

Bookmark what you're studying

Collect the events for a unit or research topic, then come back to them from the Bookmarks tab.

  • Today in Korean cultureThe home screen shows this month's Korean national holidays and memorial days, plus today's date in the lunar, Dangun, and sexagenary calendars.
  • Text size and dark modeChoose from four text sizes and switch to a dark screen.
  • Wider on tabletsIn landscape on a tablet, two neighboring eras appear side by side.
Family

Students can add their own story

The My History tab lets students record dated events from their own lives and families, and view them as a timeline. They can write in any language.

It works well as a project: students interview relatives, build a family timeline, then compare it with the history of Korea and their own country. Entries stay on the device and can be exported to a file.
